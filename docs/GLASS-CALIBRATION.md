# Calibration of the regular Liquid Glass (compositor modes 9 and 10)

What it is: `src/blur.c` draws the regular glass of macOS 26/27 (the glass the apps get from `NSGlassEffectView` / SwiftUI `glassEffect`) in software: the backdrop blurred, pulled from inside at the rim by the inner refraction (a quarter circle, amount -60 over 20 px), a colour term, the lit rim of `CASDFKeyFillHighlightEffect` (key light angle 0, fill light angle pi), a darker band inside the rim and the outer shadow. Mode 9 = shapes of 160 px or more, mode 10 = smaller ones (a narrower blur, a lighter tone).
How it was fitted (2026-11-19): the oracle is the screenshots of a real Mac (macOS 27.0, Apple silicon) in `github.com/pearOS-bsd/Solarium/captures/27.0-arm64/` (`background.png` and `regular-340x200-r60.png`, `regular-200x200-r100.png`, `regular-120x120-r60.png`, dark appearance). `tools/glass-calib/calib.c` applies `ws_glass_regions` to the raw background, `run.py` compares with a capture, `fit.py` is a coordinate descent over the knobs (environment variables `WS_G9_*`, `WS_G10_*`, read by `g9k()`), with the label of the capture masked.
Result (mean absolute difference inside the glass, 0..255): 13.3 -> 5.9 (340x200), 10 -> 6.0 (200x200 disc), 9.6 -> 5.2 (120x120 disc); the shadow, which the captures show at 0.2 of the 0.6 that the filter key says (the key is the strength before the compositing), 3.4 -> 0.55 in a ring of 45 px around.
Fitted values (dark appearance): mode 9 sigma 10.2, saturation 1.3, gain 0.88, lift 0.05, refraction x0.96, rim x0.32, ring 0.083 over 21 px; mode 10 sigma 6.65, saturation 1.43, gain 0.78, lift 0.099, rim x0.12, ring 0.05 over 4 px; shadow 0.2 (sigma 12, 8 px lower).
Limits: only the dark appearance and three sizes were available; the light appearance still uses the earlier guesses (gain 0.84, lift 0.16); the fit trades the knobs against each other (they are not Apple's values); the real glass over the panel of ControlCenter was not compared (the Mac with a GPU that could produce it, 192.168.1.103, was reached on 2026-11-19: a window capture of our layer tree showed no glass, so the glass needs the whole NSGlassEffectView / SwiftUI tree, not only the layers of CCGlass.m).
Rebuild: `gcc -O2 -I include -o calib tools/glass-calib/calib.c tools/glass-calib/surf.c src/blur.c -lm` then `WSD_APPEARANCE=dark python3 tools/glass-calib/fit.py 9` (needs the Solarium captures next to the repository or `SOLARIUM_CAPTURES`).

## Second fit: the panel of Control Center (2026-11-19, same day)
Oracle: `Solarium/captures/27.0-arm64-cc/cc-plain.png`, made on a real Mac (macOS 27.0, M1, reached over SSH) by `Solarium/tools/cc-glass-probe`: the panel and its eight controls as separate `NSGlassEffectView`s over the test background. The controls are separate glass layers: a control sees the glass of the panel as its backdrop, so `ws_glass_regions` now draws the shapes of mode 9 first and each shape of mode 10 on its own afterwards (inside an `NSGlassEffectContainerView` the shapes melt instead: `cc-container.png`, not used). A narrow blur (`WS_GS_SIGMA`, 3.9) is mixed into the wide one (18 % for the panel, 48 % for the controls, from a depth of 10 px) because the real glass keeps the pattern readable at the centre. `tools/glass-calib/fit2.py` / `fit2run.py` (multi-shape harness `calib2`). Mean absolute difference (0..255) in the interior of the controls 11.3 -> 6.2, panel alone 9.6 -> 7.2; the first three scenes stay at 6.2 / 5.9 / 9.6 (the lone 120 x 120 disc got worse, 5.2 -> 9.6: the controls were fitted nested on the panel).
Still different from the capture: the thin dark outline of the controls, the brighter top rims, no inner shadow of the real glass; dark appearance only.

## Light appearance (2026-11-19, same day)
The same two probes run on the real Mac with `PEAR_LIGHT=1` (captures in `Solarium/captures/27.0-arm64-light/`: the five scenes of glass-probe and the Control Center scene). Every knob has a value for each appearance now (`g9a()` in `blur.c`: `NAME` for dark, `NAME_L` in the environment for light; the light defaults of the fit are in a table in that function). `tools/glass-calib/fit3.py` fits them on the light captures: the sum of the four scores 215 -> 38 (340x200 9.2, 200x200 6.0, controls 12.3, panel 10.9). The glass of the light appearance is much lighter (gain 1.3, lift 0.18, saturation 1.25, a much stronger lit rim) and keeps the pattern less readable than ours; the grid lines of the backdrop show faintly in the real one. The dark fit is unchanged (6.2 / 5.9 / 9.6; controls 6.2, panel 7.2).
